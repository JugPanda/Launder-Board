# Idea Catalog

These are the current concepts. The wording below preserves the team's initial intent while making each idea easy to compare.

## 1. Lost & Found AI

**What it does:** Finds lost belongings through descriptions, photos, location, and date. Example: “black bottle with a space sticker near the library.”

**What makes it stand out:** Matches differently worded lost and found reports, showing photos and explaining why each item could be yours.

## 2. SideQuest

**What it does:** Finds something to do based on time, budget, location, and mood. Example: “I have 45 minutes, $5, and want to meet people.”

**What makes it stand out:** Turns matching activities into game-style quest cards with objectives and rewards.

## 3. Search Party

**What it does:** A detective game where players search fictional messages, photos, receipts, and witness statements to solve a mystery.

**What makes it stand out:** Search is the actual game mechanic. Players connect evidence on a board and use discoveries to unlock leads.

## 4. Thingamajig

**What it does:** Finds objects when you cannot remember their names. Example: “the little plastic thing that keeps a bread bag closed.”

**What makes it stand out:** Returns the name, picture, and related objects. Judges can challenge it with their own awkward descriptions.

## 5. ClipQuest

**What it does:** Searches recordings for moments such as “when everyone laughed after we lost” and jumps to the matching timestamp.

**What makes it stand out:** A useful creator tool with immediate visual payoff. The first demo can use a few videos with preprocessed transcripts and scene descriptions.

## 6. Screenshot Memory

**What it does:** Searches a collection of screenshots by meaning. Examples: “that purple website,” “the assignment deadline,” or “the recipe with noodles.”

**What makes it stand out:** Turns a messy screenshot folder into searchable visual memory using extracted text and image similarity.

## 7. Fridge Clutch

**What it does:** Finds meals using the ingredients, equipment, and time you actually have. Example: “eggs, rice, microwave, 10 minutes.”

**What makes it stand out:** Ranks recipes by fewest missing ingredients and prioritizes food approaching its use-by date.

## 8. QuietQuest

**What it does:** Finds places matching specific needs. Example: “quiet, dim lighting, outlets, open after 8, within a mile.”

**What makes it stand out:** Combines a map with practical details and timestamped community reports about noise and crowds.

## 9. GameDNA

**What it does:** Finds games by the exact experience someone wants. Example: “four-player co-op horror, low-end laptops, no grinding.”

**What makes it stand out:** Explains which preferences each result satisfies and lets a group combine everyone's requirements.

## 10. BorrowBox

**What it does:** Searches nearby items people are willing to lend. Example: “a screwdriver available tonight in my dorm.”

**What makes it stand out:** Solves the problem of buying something for one use. Results combine the item, distance, and borrowing window.

## 11. Meme Concierge

**What it does:** Retrieves a fitting meme from a described situation. Example: “when the code works only while the professor is watching.”

**What makes it stand out:** Searches the meaning and emotional context of memes. A live audience can submit situations during the pitch.

## 12. Second Life

**What it does:** Searches for useful projects based on things someone would otherwise throw away. Example: “cardboard boxes, an old shirt, and binder clips.”

**What makes it stand out:** Returns projects ranked by materials already owned, required tools, and completion time.
